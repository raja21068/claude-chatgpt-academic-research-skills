Seven papers, checked

What it looks like to audit an AI-generated paper: what it reports, what a reviewer should check first, and what it does not prove.

Whose papers these are

These papers were written by ScientistTwo, not by this workflow. ScientistTwo is an autonomous research system from Google Cloud AI Research, and the papers are AI-generated. Their site: scientist-two.github.io ↗.

Each card below links to a PDF. Page 1 is our own audit, written with AI help. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged. They are shared here only to help people learn how to check a paper.

Rights: ScientistTwo's page states no licence for its papers. All credit for the papers belongs to ScientistTwo. If its authors or owners want any file taken down, tell us on GitHub ↗ and we will remove it.

  • No code was run and no result was reproduced. Every number is what the source paper claims.
  • No referee model or independent reviewer was used.
  • The copies we read are marked "Anonymous authors" with an "ICLR 2025" review header, yet cite references dated 2026. The venue and date are unconfirmed, so check them with the source.
  • Do not cite these audits as publications. Use them to learn how to check a paper.

01

Others · DynaSpec-RAG

Adaptive Spectral Retrieval and Reliability-Guided Fusion for Zero-Shot Time-Series Forecasting

Open the PDF (1.7 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

Across seven forecasting benchmarks, average MSE 0.1892 versus 0.1940 for TS-RAG, with about 0.27 million trainable parameters. A 5.6% MSE improvement on GIFT-Eval without retraining.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. Call the safety fallback a validation-selected rule, not a guarantee on unseen data.
  2. Report MSE and MAE per dataset with paired uncertainty, and show how fallback choices were calibrated without test leakage.
  3. Ablate spectral splitting, boundary continuation, gating and fallback at a matched budget.
  4. Report how the retrieval index was built, the leakage safeguards, and per-series latency.

Limits

The average gain is modest and may hide regressions in some domains. A validation fallback cannot guarantee safety under any distribution shift.

02

LLMs · Mem-MaxRL

Stabilizing Reasoning Diversity in Large Language Models Through Memory-Guided Reinforcement Learning

Open the PDF (0.8 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

On a recurring 17×17 maze task, pass@256 of 66.4%, versus 59.4% for the pretrained model and 25.8% for MAXRL.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. Evaluate recurring prompts and truly unseen prompts separately.
  2. Report pass@1 next to pass@k, with confidence intervals and matched sampling budgets.
  3. Measure buffer hit rates, memory growth, retrieval cost, and success when a valid memory exists.
  4. Compare with memory-only replay, smoothed weighting alone, and equal-compute replay.

Limits

A recurring maze can favour replaying earlier successes. A high pass@k alone does not show better reasoning.

03

Foundation · UNI-XDLM

Distribution-Aware Noise Calibration for Efficient Discrete Diffusion Language Modeling

Open the PDF (0.6 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

On OpenWebText, validation perplexity 146.4 versus 155.3 for XDLM. Across seven zero-shot datasets, average perplexity 380.7 versus 432.6.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. State the assumptions of the closed-form ELBO. Separate an exact expression from exact optimisation.
  2. Separate the gains from the unigram prior, reverse-rate annealing and smoothing.
  3. Check that perplexities use compatible tokenisation, likelihood estimators, context lengths and compute.
  4. Test sensitivity to the prior corpus, vocabulary support and rare tokens.

Limits

Lower perplexity does not automatically mean better samples or reasoning. The "exact ELBO" claim needs careful qualification.

04

Interpretability · Overdamped DAVE

Efficient Vision Transformer Attribution Through Rank-Preserving Concentration Dynamics

Open the PDF (3.0 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

On ImageNet-1k with three backbones, localisation gains of up to 25.88 points (Grid Pointing Game) and 13.42 points (Energy Pointing Game). A single-sample setting runs about 93 times faster than a 100-sample setting.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. Separate rank-based deletion metrics from metrics that change under monotone rescaling.
  2. Compare the same source attribution maps before and after the concentration step.
  3. Run faithfulness tests (deletion, insertion, randomisation, distribution shift) at matched budgets.
  4. Say what hardware the latency used and whether the 93× is end to end or attribution only.

Limits

A rank-preserving step cannot improve purely rank-based measures on a fixed map. Pointing-game gains may reflect concentration, not causal faithfulness.

05

Alignment · CHVA

Hierarchical Value Representation and Calibration for Reliable Large Language Model Alignment

Open the PDF (4.2 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

Pairwise ranking accuracy up 20.3% and similarity correlation up 38.5% over base encoders. Cross-value overlap down from 0.404 to 0.119. Refusal rate falls to 51.7% under one negative-security steering condition.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. Say whether the gains are relative percentages or percentage points.
  2. State which population fits the calibration statistics, and prevent demographic test leakage.
  3. Report group-wise errors, sample sizes, uncertainty, and the ethical limits of demographic profiling.
  4. Treat the drop in refusals under extreme steering as a main safety result, not a side note.

Limits

Separating value axes does not prove normative validity or that the sample represents a population. It should not be presented as deployment-ready.

06

Neuroscience · Simplified DWGR-SVL

Simplifying Spike-Driven Vision-Language Pretraining for Efficient Open-World 3D Recognition

Open the PDF (2.4 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

Pretraining on 583,907 shapes. Zero-shot top-1 accuracy 83.75% on ModelNet40 and 41.47% on Objaverse-LVIS, which is 1.65 and 1.37 points above the stated baseline.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. Separate operation counts from measured latency, power and energy per inference.
  2. Disclose any floating-point operations in training, alignment, graph construction and inference.
  3. Run matched-data and matched-compute ablations of the Student-t MMD, patch alignment and geodesic propagation.
  4. Explain the zero-shot class splits, prompt choice, preprocessing, and any overlap with the pretraining data.

Limits

A module that only accumulates does not make the whole inference pipeline accumulation-only. State-of-the-art claims depend on comparable data and protocols.

07

Neuroscience · FCD-Engram

Curvature-Guided Parameter Editing for Selective Knowledge Removal in Large Language Models

Open the PDF (2.6 MB)Page 1 is our audit. The pages after it are the original paper, unchanged.

What the source says

On TOFU with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, overall score 0.916 versus 0.860 for the strongest tuned covariance baseline, with about 30 times fewer FLOPs than iterative methods.

Reported by the source paper. We did not run or reproduce it.

What to check first

  1. Define every part of the composite overall score and report each metric.
  2. Separate suppressing answers from removing the information inside the model. Test paraphrases and adversarial recovery.
  3. Report sequential forgetting, relearning resistance, retain-set quality, and uncertainty across targets and seeds.
  4. Count the cost of estimating covariance and Fisher information, preparing reference data and every editing pass.

Limits

Low memorisation scores and low probe decodability do not prove permanent deletion. It should not be presented as guaranteeing regulatory erasure.
