## 1. Editorial assessment

The study integrates perceptions of craftsmanship, cultural authenticity and contemporary relevance with cultural-continuity appraisals, affective response and purchase intent. In the supplied teaching account, all three antecedents exhibit positive conditional associations with the cultural-continuity variable. The submission is characterized as **empirical and conceptually incremental**, not a review article.

The central concern is **claim–measurement alignment**, followed by the interpretation of causal/serial paths in cross-sectional data, transparency of fsQCA, moderation reporting, and scale provenance. The teaching case's overall **7.0/10** is a holistic judgment and **not** the arithmetic mean of the dimension ratings or an acceptance probability.

**Priority order:** (1) establish construct content validity; (2) recover original analysis/scale provenance; (3) remove unsupported causal implications; (4) test sensitivity and resolve reporting gaps; (5) then refine the paper's contribution and presentation.

## 2. Major concerns — priority and closure

### M1 · Construct content does not fully match the PCC label

**Reported manuscript anchor:** pp. 11 and 29; lines 391–400; Appendix A.  
**Evidence reported by guide:** four items cover historical/cultural memory, continuation of values, cultural meaning, and the value of traditional culture; only one clearly addresses continuity over time.  
**Why consequential:** reliability of a set of responses does not establish that the latent construct represents *temporal cultural continuity*. The study's central interpretation depends on this meaning.  
**Minimum resolution:** map each exact administered item against the conceptual definition and original source; retain PCC only with defensible item-domain evidence, or consider the explicitly different construct *Perceived Cultural Heritage Significance (PCHS)*.  
**Closure test:** item–definition mapping is documented; definition, hypotheses, figure, tables, results, implications and limitations are consistent with the retained/revised interpretation. An item relabeling alone does not establish content validity.  
**Status:** **Open in teaching illustration** — data/source-material review not performed.

### M2 · Causal mechanisms and serial effects are overinterpreted

**Reported anchor:** pp. 4–5, 11–12, 21–22.  
**Evidence reported by guide:** product cues are discussed but not experimentally assigned or directly observed, and PLS-SEM paths are estimated from cross-sectional self-reports. Serial standardized indirect estimates are reported as **0.037 (TCA), 0.025 (PCA), 0.040 (PCR)**.  
**Why consequential:** statistical mediation under a chosen ordering does not identify causal mediation or the temporal sequence of appraisals.  
**Minimum resolution:** frame effects as model-conditional associations, describe unmeasured competing mechanisms and causal limits, and reserve mechanistic language for designs that identify the sequence.  
**Closure test:** abstract, hypotheses, discussion, tables and conclusions no longer equate cross-sectional associations with observed causal processes unless independent identification evidence is provided.  
**Status:** **Open**.

### M3 · fsQCA solution cannot be checked from the reported output

**Reported anchor:** pp. 22–24.  
**Evidence reported by guide:** ten configurations, solution coverage **0.85433** and consistency **0.824604**, with S9–S10 configuration-level consistency given as **NR**; calibration uses standardized anchors **+3 / 0 / −3**.  
**Why consequential:** incomplete configuration reporting and untested calibration/threshold choices prevent readers from evaluating the configurational findings.  
**Minimum resolution:** recover original input, calibration, truth table, necessity, minimization and sensitivity outputs; report all configurations. If reconstruction is impossible, remove unsupported conclusions rather than filling missing values.  
**Closure test:** a reproducible analysis yields all reported measures and reasonable threshold sensitivity, or the unverified analysis is withdrawn.  
**Status:** **Open — re-execution not performed**.

### M4 · Scale provenance and construct specification require inspection

**Reported anchor:** pp. 8–10, 14, 16–17; reference list p. 32.  
**Evidence reported by guide:** a cited source for the PCR modernity scale appears mismatched to the bibliography; TCA facets may behave as formative rather than interchangeable reflective indicators; TCO mixes several related cultural orientations.  
**Why consequential:** incorrect source attribution and untested measurement specification weaken construct interpretation and any moderation involving those constructs.  
**Minimum resolution:** recover original scale publications and administered wording, publish an adaptation trace, and examine defensible alternative measurement specifications using existing observations.  
**Closure test:** every indicator traces to an authentic source or disclosed new development, and sensitivity analyses support the final interpretation.  
**Status:** **Open — source publications not independently checked**.

### M5 · Moderation and prediction need fully recoverable reporting

**Reported anchor:** pp. 13–14, 18–22; lines 653–660 and 733–746.  
**Evidence reported by guide:** PK interactions have reported coefficients **0.220, 0.152 and 0.174**, with small incremental effect sizes; TCO × PCC → PAR is reported as **β = 0.484, f² = 0.196**. BI is reported with **Q² = 0.074**.  
**Why consequential:** interaction directions alone do not establish robust mechanisms; a positive prediction metric does not demonstrate strong out-of-sample purchase-intent prediction.  
**Minimum resolution:** supply integrated-model estimates, lower-order terms, interactions, confidence intervals, conditional slopes, cross-validation settings and comparable benchmark errors.  
**Closure test:** analysis scripts and tables reproduce reported coefficients, interaction plots and predictive comparisons.  
**Status:** **Open**.

### M6 · Sampling, ethical transparency and presentation consistency

**Reported anchor:** pp. 15–17, Figure 1 p. 16, discussion p. 24.  
**Evidence reported by guide:** survey reference-object wording varies between one product and product categories, and a figure retains a prior construct label. Recruitment, consent, screening and data-availability documentation require completion.  
**Why consequential:** inconsistent reference objects may create unexplained heterogeneity; mislabeling and incomplete provenance impair verification.  
**Minimum resolution:** describe exact respondent instructions, panel selection, screening, recruitment and dates, complete documented ethics statements, and harmonize labels across all materials.  
**Closure test:** questionnaire/source records and a full-paper terminology audit agree with the updated methods, figures and disclosures.  
**Status:** **Open**.

## 4. Construct decision: keep, clarify or rename

| Construct | Teaching recommendation | New data immediately? |
|---|---|---|
| TCA · Traditional Craftsmanship Appraisal | Retain; test measurement-specification alternatives | No, not for initial sensitivity analysis |
| PCA · Perceived Cultural Authenticity | Clarify as *respondent-inferred* authenticity | No |
| PCR · Perceived Contemporary Relevance | Verify scale origin and item adaptation | No |
| PCC · Perceived Cultural Continuity | **Conditional:** consider *Perceived Cultural Heritage Significance* only if theory meaning is revised | Not for reinterpretation alone |
| PK · Product Knowledge | Clarify as *Subjective Product Knowledge* | No |
| TCO · Traditional Cultural Orientation | Retain provisionally; examine item/domain heterogeneity | Not initially |
| PAR · Positive Affective Response | Retain | No |
| BI · Buying Intent | Retain; do not describe as actual purchasing | No |

**Renaming is a theory decision, not a find-and-replace operation.** Changes must propagate through construct definition → hypotheses → model figure → reported results → implications → limitations. Cronbach's alpha or HTMT cannot establish content validity.

## 5. Revision triage: when existing data suffice

| Revision route | What to do | Evidence required to close |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Reporting correction** | Align terminology, disclose recruitment, improve tables | Changed text/figure and traceable supporting source |
| **B · Existing-data reanalysis** | Alternative measurement models, conditional slopes, fsQCA rerun, predictive checks | Rerunnable scripts, logs and numerical agreement |
| **C · Source audit** | Trace scale and bibliographic sources to actual instruments | Original publication, original items, adaptation table |
| **D · Independent content validation** | Expert ratings or validation sample for disputed construct meaning | Documented design, ratings and explicit interpretive limits |
| **E · New measurement/design** | Test actual preservation, experimentally assigned product cues, factual knowledge or verified purchases | New measures, appropriate design and recorded outcomes |

**Decision:** do **not** collect new respondents just to make the paper appear stronger. First use the existing data to close A–C issues. Collect new data only when the revised claim genuinely requires a property absent from the instrument (such as causal cue effects or real purchases).
